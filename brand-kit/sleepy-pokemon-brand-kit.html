Sleepy Pokemon Brand Kit Draft

A practical visual direction deck for the hybrid brand: Moonlit Binder presence, Cozy Checklist clarity, and the locked Sparkle logo direction.

01. Brand Cover

Sleepy Pokemon

A collector-built guide to peaceful, sleepy, and sleep-adjacent Pokemon card art.

Chosen direction: two stacked cards, one small four-point sparkle, and a warm-but-useful product voice.

02. Brand Essence

All the Sleepy Pokemon. Curated with care.

Sleepy Pokemon is a quiet archive of tiny peaceful scenes, kept by someone who noticed. It should feel complete, but never cold; useful, but never generic.

The emotional promise is not just finding cards. It is finding the little restful moments hiding inside a huge collecting world.

Completeness

Every sleepy and sleep-adjacent card deserves a place.

Human curation

Cards belong because the artwork says something restful.

Serendipity

Random browsing should feel like opening a binder to a perfect page.

Respect

No ads, no paywalls, no sponsored placement, no visual noise over the cards.

03. Logo Direction

Stacked cards, with one small sparkle.

The locked mark is soft, clear, and card-first. The sparkle signals a rare find or a small moment of discovery without over-explaining the word “sleepy.”

This is a direction image, not final vector production art.

04. Voice System

Warm, specific, collector-to-collector.

The voice should sound like Comet actually looked at the card art and made a tiny judgment call. Gentle humor is welcome. Generic cozy filler is not.

Comet's voice is observational first, charming second.

Say this

"Technically awake. Spiritually asleep."

Say this

"This one counts because the whole scene feels seconds from sleep."

Not this

"Cozy vibes for your sleepy Pokemon journey."

Not this

"Unlock premium collecting with sponsored recommendations."

05. Color System

Night archive, paper warmth, useful accents.

The palette should not become one-note purple or sleepy-app blue. It needs enough range for product UI, card framing, and rare editorial moments.

Use dark colors for atmosphere, cream for readability, and accents only where they help someone browse.

06. Typography

Clean product type with one human accent.

Readable, friendly, and useful. Save the handmade feeling for rare Comet's Notes and small moments.

Priority: mobile readability, fast scanning, no precious tiny type.

Useful first, quietly charming second.

Search, filters, chips, and random browsing should feel like collection tools from a lovingly maintained binder.

08. Card Detail Recipe

The card is the hero. The brand is the frame.

Every detail page should make the artwork inspectable, then explain why it belongs in the collection.

Metadata stays structured and crawlable. Commentary stays human and optional.

09. Comet's Notes

Rare notes should feel like margin discoveries.

Comet's Notes are not flavor text for every card. They are small human moments that make the archive feel lovingly kept.

Use when a card has a debatable inclusion, a special observation, or a delightful art-specific reason to call out.

"Sleep-adjacent, but emotionally undeniable."
"The whole scene feels like it is trying not to wake anyone up."
10. Homepage Direction

Promise, search, serendipity, index.

The homepage should immediately say what the site is, make search obvious, keep random browsing delightful, and preserve crawlable access to the full card collection.

No cinematic landing page that delays the cards. The first screen should already feel like the collection.

12. Restraint and Don'ts

Protect the cards, the search intent, and the trust.

The brand succeeds when it makes the collection feel loved without making it harder to browse, index, or believe.

SEO architecture, card metadata, and plain utility are part of the brand because they keep the promise honest.

×

No official Pokemon imitation.
Avoid franchise-like logo colors, marks, and visual language.

×

No generic cozy filler.
Every line should connect to cards, art, collecting, or Comet's judgment.

×

No monetization-first layout.
Support links stay quiet, optional, and never ranking-driven.

×

No landing page that hides the collection.
The cards and search experience should show up immediately.
